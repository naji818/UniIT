# Title options

YouTube truncates around **60 characters** in most feeds (the hard limit is 100).
Character counts below include spaces.

| # | Title | Chars | Character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **What Nobody Tells You About Studying Computer Science** | 53 | Curiosity gap, not clickbait. Broadest appeal. |
| 2 | **Your Computer Science Degree Is Not the Finish Line** | 51 | States the thesis outright. Confident, a little confrontational. |
| 3 | Day One of Your CS Degree — What Nobody Tells You | 49 | Speaks directly to a first-year starting today. |
| 4 | You Entered University to Study Computer Science. Now What? | 59 | Closest to your original working title. |
| 5 | Computer Science at University: Are You Ready for the Future? | 61 | Most descriptive, least punchy. Slight truncation risk. |

## Recommended pairing

**Thumbnail B** (`YOUR CS DEGREE IS / NOT THE FINISH LINE`)
**Title 1** (*What Nobody Tells You About Studying Computer Science*)

The reason: a thumbnail and a title should say **two different things**. Pairing
Thumbnail B with Title 2 would print nearly the same sentence twice and waste
half your space. Letting the thumbnail state the thesis while the title opens a
question gives the viewer two separate reasons to click.

If you prefer the softer, warmer read for your nephew, invert it:
**Thumbnail C** + **Title 2**.

## Avoid

- ALL CAPS titles, and more than one exclamation mark — both read as spam.
- "SHOCKING", "THEY DON'T WANT YOU TO KNOW", "MUST WATCH". The film's authority
  comes from being measured; the title should match it.
- Putting the runtime or "Part 1" in the title. Neither helps anyone find it.
